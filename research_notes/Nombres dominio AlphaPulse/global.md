# Globally usable names and exact .com domains

## Which names are easy to pronounce and spell in Spanish and English?

### Takeaway
The strongest bilingual construction is a familiar English market term (`Trend`, `Stock`, `Signal`, or `Chart`) plus a short Spanish word with simple consonant-vowel spelling. `TrendFoco`, `TrendNivel`, and `SignalFaro` offer the best balance of meaning, spelling, and credible analytical-software positioning.

### Cited Findings
- `foco` means a concentrated light and, figuratively, a place where something is concentrated; this supports an analysis/focus metaphor, although the dictionary also gives `foco de infecciones` as an example of a negative secondary sense. Pronunciation guide: Spanish `TREND FO-ko`; likely English `trend FOH-koh`. Spelling is simple in both languages. — [RAE: foco](https://dle.rae.es/foco)
- `nivel` means height, a measure against a scale, category, or rank. Those meanings map directly to price levels and ranked Candidates without implying execution or returns. Pronunciation guide: Spanish `TREND nee-BEL`; likely English `trend nee-VEL`. — [RAE: nivel](https://dle.rae.es/nivel)
- `faro` means lighthouse and, figuratively, something that illuminates an issue or guides understanding. That makes `SignalFaro` a defensible “signal beacon” metaphor. Pronunciation guide: Spanish `seeg-NAL FA-ro`; likely English `SIG-nuhl FAR-oh`. — [RAE: faro](https://dle.rae.es/faro)
- `senda` means path or route. It is easy to spell and say (`SEN-da`) in both languages, but its connection to screening is more metaphorical than `foco` or `nivel`. — [RAE: senda](https://dle.rae.es/senda)
- The product is an EOD analytical Screener that returns ranked Candidates and does not execute orders, provide intraday data, or promise performance; names using `Trend`, `Stock`, `Signal`, and `Chart` accurately reflect that scope. — [ChartScreenPlus build brief](../../docs/build-brief.md); [project vocabulary](../../CONTEXT.md)

### Inferences
- Best bilingual clarity: `TrendFoco`, `TrendNivel`, `SignalFaro`.
- Best immediate stock-market clarity: `StockNivel`, followed by `StockFoco`; English `stock` is globally recognizable, but some Spanish speakers may naturally pronounce an initial `e` (“estock”).
- Best softer brand options: `TrendSenda` and `ChartSenda`; both are pronounceable but need a descriptor such as “EOD stock screener” during launch.
- `ChartVera` is compact and credible-looking, but `Vera` has variable English pronunciations and the Spanish `v/b` sound makes spoken-to-written recall weaker than the top choices.

### Gaps
- Pronunciation judgments are qualitative rather than the result of user testing. A final decision should include short read-aloud and “hear it, then spell it” tests with native speakers from more than one Spanish-speaking market and at least one US-English group.

## Are there obvious finance/software companies, products, negative meanings, or trademark risks?

### Takeaway
Exact-name web checks did not surface an obvious finance or software product for the recommended eight, but this is only a basic collision screen. Component words are descriptive or commonly used, and an attorney-led clearance search for confusingly similar marks and relevant classes remains necessary before launch.

### Cited Findings
- Exact quoted searches produced no parsed result identifying a company or product named `TrendFoco`, `TrendNivel`, `SignalFaro`, `StockNivel`, `StockFoco`, `TrendSenda`, `ChartSenda`, or `ChartVera` at the check time. Search evidence: [TrendFoco](https://duckduckgo.com/?q=%22TrendFoco%22), [TrendNivel](https://duckduckgo.com/?q=%22TrendNivel%22), [SignalFaro](https://duckduckgo.com/?q=%22SignalFaro%22), [StockNivel](https://duckduckgo.com/?q=%22StockNivel%22), [StockFoco](https://duckduckgo.com/?q=%22StockFoco%22), [TrendSenda](https://duckduckgo.com/?q=%22TrendSenda%22), [ChartSenda](https://duckduckgo.com/?q=%22ChartSenda%22), and [ChartVera](https://duckduckgo.com/?q=%22ChartVera%22).
- USPTO says a proper clearance search should look for confusingly similar marks, not only exact matches, and should include federal records plus internet/common-law sources. — [USPTO: comprehensive clearance search](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/comprehensive-clearance-search-similar-trademarks)
- The official USPTO database is the appropriate US federal trademark source, but its current search application requires JavaScript and does not provide a stable, candidate-specific evidence URL from this research environment. — [USPTO trademark search](https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/); [USPTO search guidance](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search)
- `FARO` is already the corporate name of FARO Technologies, a software-and-hardware company in 3D measurement. This is not an exact `SignalFaro` collision or a stock-screener product, but it raises the component-word risk from low to low-medium. — [FARO](https://www.faro.com/)
- `foco` has a benign “focus/concentration/light” meaning but can also describe a center of infection; the latter is a mild negative association rather than an obvious brand blocker. — [RAE: foco](https://dle.rae.es/foco)
- `nivel`, `faro`, and `senda` have neutral or positive dictionary meanings relevant to levels, guidance, and a path; none promises returns or implies brokerage/execution. — [RAE: nivel](https://dle.rae.es/nivel); [RAE: faro](https://dle.rae.es/faro); [RAE: senda](https://dle.rae.es/senda)

### Inferences
- **Low preliminary risk:** `TrendFoco`, `TrendNivel`, `StockNivel`, `StockFoco`, `TrendSenda`, `ChartSenda`. Exact composites appear distinctive even though their components are descriptive.
- **Low-medium preliminary risk:** `SignalFaro`, because FARO is an established technology company; `ChartVera`, because Vera is a common personal name and brand element and has pronunciation ambiguity.
- More descriptive names may be easier to understand but harder to protect strongly as trademarks. `TrendFoco` and `TrendNivel` are the best product-fit compromises, not necessarily the strongest marks in isolation.
- No candidate makes an AI claim, implies a broker or execution venue, or promises profit. This is consistent with the product's analytical-only positioning. — [ChartScreenPlus build brief](../../docs/build-brief.md)

### Gaps
- No legal opinion or comprehensive trademark clearance was performed. The official USPTO interface could be reached but its JavaScript results could not be captured as stable, candidate-specific citations; therefore this report does **not** claim “no USPTO matches.”
- EUIPO, WIPO, UKIPO, and national Spanish/Latin American trademark registers were not exhaustively searched. Global use requires class-specific professional clearance, especially for software/SaaS and financial-analysis services.
- App stores, social handles, corporate registries, and phonetic variants were not exhaustively cleared.
